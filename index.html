03 · September 25th–27th

The weekend of
the wedding

The wedding falls on La Mercè weekend, when Barcelona celebrates its patron saint with street festivities across the city.

There are traditional Catalan events, activities and concerts that we’d love to share with you. You don’t need to plan around them; you’ll most likely run into the celebrations if you’re walking downtown.

We’ll coordinate which events to attend together on Saturday afternoon and Sunday as the date gets closer. The full programme is still to be released.

04 · Good to know

Practical
information

01

What’s the dress code?

Cocktail. Black tie or a tuxedo is optional if you own one and feel like getting fancy; dressing less formally is also completely fine if that makes you more comfortable.

If you plan to join us after 23:00, bring comfortable clothes and shoes. You can leave heels or jackets at the hotel, and we’ll make sure they get back to you afterwards.

02

Food requirements

If you have a food allergy or don’t eat certain foods, please let us know beforehand. If you’ve been particularly vocal about your nut allergy, we are already aware.

03

Gifts?

If you’re coming from abroad—especially the Americans—we don’t want to receive a single dime from you. Given our geographically challenged lifestyle, we’ve opted against a traditional wedding list. We really don’t want to take a waffle maker with us in our carry-on bag.

If you really want to bring something, choose a small gift that meaningfully describes our relationship.

05 · A few opinions about

Barcelona

Our very subjective guide to getting around, staying safe and eating well.

Moving around

Walk or take the metro.

Those are the easiest ways to move around the city. The metro is expected to run continuously through La Mercè weekend, including overnight.

From the airport

Aerobús is easy.

A1 serves Terminal 1 and A2 serves Terminal 2; both run 24/7 to Plaça Catalunya. FREENOW for local taxis and Uber also operate in Barcelona.

Safety

Watch your belongings.

Barcelona is very safe in terms of violent crime, but pickpocketing is extremely common. Keep valuables out of outer backpack pockets and never leave anything unattended. Yes, it can happen to you: several wedding guests already hold personal records on PickPocketBench.

What to order

I

Tapas of many sorts

You’ll try plenty at the wedding. Look out for truita de patates / tortilla de patatas, cured meats such as pernil or jamón and fuet, plus seafood including chocos, pop / pulpo and boquerones / seitons. They usually come with pa amb tomàquet.

II

Paella & fideuà

Rice or short noodles cooked with seafood. Go somewhere that specialises in rice rather than ordering one at random on La Rambla. Arròs negre—black with cuttlefish ink—is our favourite variation.

III

Something sweet

Crema catalana, mel i mató, ensaïmada, flan, and chocolate with churros. The chocolate should be thick enough to eat with a spoon.
